# Hi, I'm Kazu Yamagiwa

I study **computation through evolving systems** — especially how simple local rules on graphs and networks produce structure over time.

By day I work as a **materials researcher** (experimental and analytical / machine learning). That practice keeps me grounded in measurement and data; what I most want to explore openly is the computational side.

---

### Focus: rule-based graph evolution

My main open research thread is **[`graphnet-automata`](https://github.com/kazuyamagiwa/graphnet-automata)**: growing networks by applying cellular-automaton-like updates to adjacency matrices, then watching communities and morphologies emerge (bonding / anti-bonding patterns, whip-like structures, and related forms).

Inspired by Wolfram-style ideas about physics-from-rules, but implemented as a concrete Python experiment (NetworkX, NumPy, Numba).

I am working toward writing this line of work up more formally (including for arXiv).
